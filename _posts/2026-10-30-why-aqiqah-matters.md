Every parent wants to give their child the best possible start. In Islam, aqiqah is part of that start — and understanding why it matters helps a family treat it as more than a formality. This article is part of our [complete guide to aqiqah](/blog/aqiqah-complete-guide/).

## Protection for a vulnerable new life

The theme of protection runs clearly through the sources. In the hadith of Salman ibn Amir (RA), recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ tied the sacrifice to removing harm from the newborn:

And in the hadith of Samurah ibn Jundub (RA), every child is described as "held in pledge" by his aqiqah. Scholars such as Ibn al-Qayyim explained this to mean the sacrifice ransoms the child — just as Allah ransomed Isma'il with a ram — lifting a hindrance and inviting Allah's care. For a parent holding a days-old baby, this is a deeply reassuring act.

## A declaration of gratitude

Aqiqah says, in action, that this child is a gift from Allah, received with thanks rather than taken for granted. Beginning a child's life with gratitude invites increase, for Allah rewards the thankful. It sets a tone the parents hope will shape the child's whole life.

## Following the Prophet ﷺ

Aqiqah matters because it is a direct Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ, who performed it for his own grandsons Hasan and Husayn (RA). To begin a child's life by reviving his practice is itself a blessing, and a quiet act of love for him.

## A gift to the community

The meat of the aqiqah reaches relatives, neighbours, and the poor, so a family's private joy becomes a source of good for others. In a world that often celebrates privately, aqiqah turns a happy moment outward — feeding the hungry in the name of a new life.

## Why it is worth prioritising

Because it is not, for most, a strict obligation, aqiqah can be easy to postpone indefinitely. But its combination of protection, gratitude, obedience, and charity makes it one of the most meaningful things a parent can do in a child's first days. Even where finances are tight, many families find a way — and those who truly cannot are excused and can perform it later.

## In short

Aqiqah matters because it wraps a newborn's earliest days in worship, thankfulness, protection, and generosity — the very values you hope will define the rest of their life.
