Many parents perform aqiqah knowing it is a good thing to do, without ever learning what the word actually means or why Islam places such value on it. Understanding both deepens the act and turns a routine into genuine worship. This article sits within our [complete guide to aqiqah](/blog/aqiqah-complete-guide/).

## The meaning of the word "aqiqah"

The term *aqiqah* comes from the Arabic root *'aqqa*, which carries the sense of "cutting." In its earliest use, the word referred to the hair on a newborn's head — the hair that is shaved shortly after birth. From there, the word extended to the sacrifice offered on the same occasion, because the two acts traditionally happen together on the seventh day. So the very name of the ritual ties it to the baby's first days of life.

## Why aqiqah is important

Aqiqah's importance rests on several pillars, each giving the act real weight in a believer's life.

### 1. It is an expression of gratitude

Children are described in the Quran as an adornment of the life of this world (Surah al-Kahf 18:46). Aqiqah is the family's way of thanking Allah for entrusting them with this blessing — responding to a gift with worship rather than taking it for granted.

### 2. It follows the Prophet ﷺ

Aqiqah is not a cultural custom; it is a prophetic practice. The Prophet ﷺ taught it, encouraged it, and performed it himself for his grandsons Hasan and Husayn (RA). Following his example is itself a source of reward.

### 3. It carries protection and blessing

In the hadith of Samurah ibn Jundub (RA), every child is described as "held in pledge" by his aqiqah:

Scholars such as Ibn al-Qayyim explained this to mean that the sacrifice frees the child from a kind of hindrance and invites Allah's protection and blessing upon them — which is why parents are encouraged not to neglect it.

### 4. It builds community

The meat of the aqiqah is shared with relatives, neighbours, and especially the poor. A private family joy becomes a public good, and feeding the hungry is among the most praised acts in Islam.

## A meaning worth remembering

Put simply, aqiqah means welcoming a child with gratitude, obedience, protection, and generosity — all in one act. That combination is why the scholars of the Sunnah have always treated it as a cherished and emphasised practice, rather than a mere formality.
