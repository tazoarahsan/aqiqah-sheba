Aqiqah and Qurbani are both Islamic sacrifices, and because both involve slaughtering a halal animal and sharing the meat, they are often confused. They are, however, two distinct acts of worship with different reasons behind them. Here is how they compare. See also our [complete guide to aqiqah](/blog/aqiqah-complete-guide/).

## Different reasons and occasions

**Qurbani** (also called *udhiyah*) is tied to the days of Eid al-Adha. It commemorates the willingness of Prophet Ibrahim (AS) to sacrifice his son in obedience to Allah, and Allah's ransoming of him with a ram. It is performed during a fixed window each year.

**Aqiqah** is tied to the birth of a child. Its reason is gratitude for a newborn, and it is offered ideally on the seventh day after the child is born.

## Different frequency

Qurbani can be offered **every year** by those who are able. Aqiqah is a **one-time** act for each child — offered once, not annually.

## Different quantity

For Qurbani, one sheep or goat is for one person or household, and a cow or camel is shared among seven. For aqiqah, the Sunnah is two animals for a boy and one for a girl:

Where two are not possible for a boy, one suffices and the aqiqah is still valid.

## What they share

Both are acts of drawing near to Allah through sacrifice; both require a halal animal that meets the conditions of a valid sacrifice; and in both, the meat is eaten, gifted, and given in charity.

## Can one animal count for both?

This question arises when a baby's seventh day falls near Eid al-Adha. The scholars differ. The Hanafi school permits allocating a share of a shared sacrificial animal (such as a cow of seven shares) for aqiqah alongside Qurbani shares, since both seek nearness to Allah. The Maliki and relied-upon Shafi'i view is to keep them separate, because each has its own distinct cause. The safest course, where possible, is two separate sacrifices; a family in doubt should consult a trusted local scholar.

## In summary

Qurbani honours a great prophetic legacy each year; aqiqah welcomes and gives thanks for a newborn, once per child. Different reasons, same underlying spirit of devotion and charity.
