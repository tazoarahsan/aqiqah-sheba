Aqiqah has a history that reaches back before Islam, but the Prophet ﷺ reshaped it from a superstitious custom into a pure act of worship. Understanding this history shows how carefully Islam purified the traditions it inherited. This article is part of our [complete guide to aqiqah](/blog/aqiqah-complete-guide/).

## Before Islam: a custom mixed with superstition

The Arabs of the pre-Islamic period did perform a sacrifice when a child was born — but they would smear the blood of the slaughtered animal onto the baby's head, a practice rooted in superstition. This is described in the narration of Buraydah (RA), who recalled that in the days of ignorance, they would slaughter a sheep for a newborn boy and daub his head with its blood.

## The prophetic reform

When Islam came, the Prophet ﷺ kept what was good in the custom — the sacrifice and the shaving of the head — and removed what was corrupt. Instead of smearing blood, families were taught to apply a pleasant scent (such as saffron) to the child's head. In the words of Buraydah's narration, after Islam they would slaughter the sheep, shave the child's head, and apply saffron to it. In one move, the act was cleansed of superstition and redirected toward gratitude to Allah.

The Prophet ﷺ also abolished the old pagan sacrifices known as *fara'* and *atira*, as reported by Abu Hurayrah (RA) in Sahih al-Bukhari — further distinguishing the pure Sunnah of aqiqah from the practices of ignorance.

## The prophetic example

The Prophet ﷺ then established aqiqah firmly through his own actions, performing it for his beloved grandsons Hasan and Husayn (RA):

He also taught its details in narrations such as those of Samurah ibn Jundub and Umm Kurz (RA), and his daughter Fatimah (RA) gave the weight of her children's shaved hair in silver as charity.

## Passed down through the generations

From the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions, aqiqah passed to every generation of Muslims after them, carried across the Muslim world and preserved in the books of hadith and fiqh. Its history is, in a sense, a small picture of Islam itself: an older custom stripped of falsehood and raised into a beautiful act of worship and thankfulness.

## In short

Aqiqah did not begin with Islam, but it was perfected by it. What was once a blood-smearing superstition became an act of gratitude, charity, and love — a transformation that still blesses Muslim families today.
