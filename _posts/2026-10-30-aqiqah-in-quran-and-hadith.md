A thoughtful parent will want to know exactly where aqiqah comes from in the sources. This article gives the honest, complete answer — including a point many are surprised by. It is part of our [complete guide to aqiqah](/blog/aqiqah-complete-guide/).

## Is aqiqah mentioned in the Quran?

In honesty, **aqiqah is not named in the Quran.** There is no verse that mentions the word or commands the act. Aqiqah is established entirely by the authentic Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ. Any article claiming a specific Quranic verse "about aqiqah" should be treated with caution.

That said, the Quran provides the foundation on which the Sunnah stands. Allah commands the believers to follow whatever the Messenger ﷺ brings:

> وَمَا آتَاكُمُ الرَّسُولُ فَخُذُوهُ وَمَا نَهَاكُمْ عَنْهُ فَانْتَهُوا
>
> "Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it; and whatever he forbids you, refrain from it." — Surah al-Hashr 59:7

It is on this basis that the Prophet's ﷺ practices — including aqiqah — become part of the religion. The Quran also frames children as a blessing worth being grateful for (Surah al-Kahf 18:46), giving context to the act.

## The hadith that establish aqiqah

The real evidence for aqiqah is a set of authentic narrations. Here are the key ones.

### 1. The foundational hadith (Samurah ibn Jundub, RA)

This single hadith gives the act, its timing, and the companion Sunnahs of naming and shaving.

   meaning="With the boy is his aqiqah, so shed blood on his behalf and remove the harm from him."

### 4. The Prophet's ﷺ own practice (Ibn Abbas, RA)

This is the clearest proof of all, because it is his action.

## A note on weak narrations

Some commonly repeated details rest on weak narrations and should not be presented as firm proof — for example, the specific pattern of the 14th and 21st days (from Buraydah, RA) is graded weak. Being honest about this protects both the reader and the credibility of the knowledge.

## In summary

Aqiqah's authority comes from the authentic Sunnah, framed by the Quran's general command to follow the Prophet ﷺ. The evidence is strong, clear, and well-documented — even though the word itself never appears in the Quran.
