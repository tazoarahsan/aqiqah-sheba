Aqiqah is the sacrifice of a permissible animal — usually a sheep or goat — offered in gratitude to Allah for the birth of a child. It is one of the first acts of worship a parent carries out on a newborn's behalf, and it revives a cherished practice of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. If you are hearing the word "aqiqah" for the first time, this article explains exactly what it is. For the full picture, see our [complete guide to aqiqah](/blog/aqiqah-complete-guide/).

## The simple definition

In the language of the Sharia, aqiqah is a sacrifice made specifically on the occasion of a child's birth. It is distinct from Qurbani, which is tied to Eid al-Adha. Aqiqah is performed once for each child, ideally on the seventh day after birth, and the meat is shared with family, friends, and the poor.

## Where the word comes from

The word *aqiqah* derives from an Arabic root meaning "to cut." It originally referred to the hair on a newborn's head that is shaved after birth, and over time it came to mean the sacrifice offered on that same occasion. This is why, to this day, aqiqah and the shaving of the baby's head are closely linked in the Sunnah.

## What the Prophet ﷺ taught about it

This single narration gives the act, its timing, and the companion Sunnahs of naming and shaving. The Prophet ﷺ also performed aqiqah for his own grandsons, Hasan and Husayn (RA).

## The key facts at a glance

* **What:** a sheep or goat (the majority also permit a cow or camel) sacrificed for a newborn.
* **When:** the seventh day after birth is best; it can be done later if missed.
* **How many:** two animals for a boy and one for a girl, with one sufficing where two are not possible.
* **The meat:** eaten by the family, gifted to relatives and neighbours, and given to the poor.
* **On the same day:** the child is named and the head is shaved.

## Is it obligatory?

Aqiqah is a highly encouraged act. The majority of scholars consider it an emphasised Sunnah, while the Hanafi school, dominant in Bangladesh, considers it permissible and commendable. No school criticises the family who performs it, and for the majority there is no sin on a family genuinely unable to do so. We cover this in detail in [Is aqiqah fard, wajib, or Sunnah?](/blog/is-aqiqah-fard-wajib-sunnah/)

## In short

Aqiqah is a short, beautiful act of gratitude at the start of a child's life — thanking Allah for the gift of the child, seeking blessing and protection for them, and feeding the community. It is a Sunnah well worth fulfilling, whether performed at home or arranged through a trusted service.
